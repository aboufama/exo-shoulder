Score: higher is better, 100 = every target met, 0 = nothing works. It is the share of three targets a design meets - metal 2-14 mm from the skin (target 90 % of the metal), following the arm (95 % of motion frames), reaching everyday poses (90 %) - minus a penalty for any pose where metal would touch the skin.

Two ways to fail

Each dot is a design. Right = moves with the arm everywhere; up = stays close to the skin. A winner sits in the green corner. Most designs do one or the other.

Score vs simplicity

Each dot is a design whose parts have been counted. Right = higher score; down = simpler. The simplicity number is points: 0 = the simplest buildable chain (three hinges, three links), and every extra freedom, slider, sensor, loop or part adds points. The goal is the bottom-right corner: once scores reach 95, the simpler design wins. The number is shown beside the score and never changes it.

Leaderboard

Every design that has a measured number. Ranked by the independently verified number (frozen shape, exact bench); a score marked "self-measured" is the design's own frozen claim, and one marked "fast search" is an unverified quick-bench number that typically drops 5-14 points when verified - those rank below every verified design. Dead and refuted designs rank below the live ones: their number is often a best-case bound measured without the hardware the design would need. A design with 4 joints is marked as such and is not ranked for the lead (the 3-joint designs are the real leaders). "Flagged" means the alignment auditor found the score gain is a measurement artefact rather than a better exo (for example metal moved into the 1-2 mm shell next to the skin); it does not count as verified until resolved. Motion: each design through the same arm loop, at one scale. "Checking" means an independent agent is re-measuring it. "Stopped early" means it was called dead while its search was still improving, so it is being re-opened. Names: R = a hinge (revolute joint), P = a slider; "3R" = three hinges in a row (3 degrees of freedom); "spherical" = all hinge axes meet at the centre of the shoulder joint; "offset" = they do not; "3-RRR" = three hinged legs side by side. Simplicity: points for every freedom, slider, sensor, loop and part beyond the simplest buildable chain (three hinges, three links = 0), lower = simpler; hover a number for where its points come from. It is not part of the score.
