### L3. Overhead: CONFIRMED in part

Confirmed from the probe JSONs:

- Of the 18 taken overhead poses, longdyad has 13 with metal < 2 mm (8 clip, 5 workspace) and parallelogram 15.
- Longdyad's deepest contact is -4.61 mm at plane 30 / elevation 165 / axial -60.
- The raised-arm reach is 45 / 113, 42 / 113 and 46 / 113.

Two statements are wrong:
1. **"On 71 % of overhead poses the wearer's arm meets the J2 fold stop."** The probe's own `ws_overhead_why_not`
   attributes the 20 lost overhead poses to fold 14, slide 4 and contact 2, for longdyad and parallelogram alike. The
   fold stop is 14 / 28 = 50 %. Seven of the 20 losses (25 % of overhead) are the cuff slide's travel and contact.
   That is a different lever (a longer cuff slide, clearing hub K), so the attribution matters for round 9.
2. **"R4's name says the opposite of the measured overhead reach."** The scorecard row reads `Overhead works (Raise
   overhead clip score) (14 mm band)`. That is exactly REQUIREMENTS.md R4: "Raise overhead" clip score, >= 80 %.
   - The label is accurate. Only HANDOFF's shorthand "R4 overhead 71 %" is loose.
   - A share of overhead workspace reach next to R4 is still worth adding (P2), but the rename is cosmetic.

The substance stands: overhead is the user's first priority, it is under-weighted, and most taken overhead poses graze.

### L5. sidecar-parallelogram: the facts hold; the causal story and the "override" do not

Confirmed:

- Clip floor histogram: 19 poses at 1-2 mm and 0 at 6-10 mm for parallelogram, against 10 and 13 for longdyad.

Not supported:
- **"The tie-break selects the tie that hugs the skin hardest."** The 209 ties span fit in-band 0.9000-0.9049, a 0.5 %
  spread (`docs/concepts/sweeps/sidecar-parallelogram-knuckle-3r.json`). Maximising in-band 2-14 also penalises metal
  below 2 mm, so the rule does not reward hugging by construction. Whether the chosen tie grazes more than its
  siblings is an empirical question. The row itself says the tie spread was never measured.
  - I wore two other ties frozen on Bench(110,360) exact through the claim's verifier harness, unchanged: k 947 (the
    lowest fit in-band, 0.900) and k 2797 (the median, 0.9018). Results are in the addendum below.
- **"The screener overrode its judge's kill gate"** as a gaming element. The gate was a go / no-go screen on the
  *untuned* per-pose placement, meant to decide whether a CMA was worth the money. The agent ran the CMA anyway and
  wrote the failed gate, its number and its reason into the row. No score was inflated. The process point (a judge's
  gate is the lead's to override, and the override is logged) is fair. It is not evidence that the number is gamed.

The action "block any CAD swap to parallelogram" costs nothing and is right on the merits: longdyad wins on plain, at
1 mm, at 2 mm and at 2 mm held out. So nothing is lost either way.

### The tool's "gamed" verdicts from the 2 mm shell: FALSE POSITIVE as "gamed"

`near_touch` (gamed above 25 points in the 1-2 mm shell) and `dominance` (gamed if a design beats frontload on the
plain gap and loses at 2 mm) put parallelogram, roller-flank and k-rim in "gamed". Four reasons make this crying wolf.

1. **No rule makes 1-2 mm metal contact.**
   - The user's ruling and CLAUDE.md say near-touch is metal < 1 mm.
   - REQUIREMENTS R1 makes 2-14 mm a share (>= 90 %), so up to 10 % of the metal may sit outside the band, under 2 mm
     included ("2 mm keeps the liner / clearance").
   - "Skin bulges, so 1-2 mm on the model is contact" (ALIGNMENT.md section 1) is asserted, not measured. Nothing in
     FINDINGS sizes the skin model's error at the deltoid.
   - The task brief says a tightening is a proposal with its measured effect, decided by the lead or the user.
     Labelling designs "gamed" by it applies the rule before anyone has adopted it.
2. **The 2 mm reading sits on a cliff.** At 3 mm every design scores 150-165, so the 2-3 mm band holds about half of
   all poses on every design.
   - One clip pose crossing 2 mm moves the gap by 0.91 (follow) + 0.27 (contact).
   - roller-flank's "loss" to frontload at 2 mm (2.84) is about 2-3 poses. A gamed / clean line drawn there flips on
     a sub-millimetre change in hub crown height that is the same O20 part on every design.
3. **The `dominance` reason text is contradicted by the auditor's own probe.** It says "the gain is metal moved into
   the 1-2 mm shell". The probe shows the sidecars' gain over frontload is the rod drive's far metal:
   - samples > 20 mm fall from 8.3 % to 2.8 %;
   - workspace in-band rises 0.827 -> 0.870 / 0.874;
   - frontload's rod is 0 % in band, at a median 22 mm.

   ALIGNMENT.md finding 7 and L2 both call this gain real. More grazing poses is a side effect that comes with a real
   gain, and its size is a judgement for the wearer. That is a trade-off ("watch"), not a fake improvement ("gamed").
4. **"gamed" is meant to block.** By the ledger's definition a gamed design "cannot be called verified, a winner or a
   leader". Applied to the 1-2 mm shell, it would block any future design that brings metal closer to the 10 mm aim
   at the cost of a few grazing poses. That is the direction the user asked for.

**Ruling.** parallelogram, roller-flank and k-rim go from **gamed** to **watch** in the ledger, with the rulings files
`docs/alignment/<id>.rulings.json`.
- The 2 mm shell numbers stay on file and in the review.
- They should go to the lead as a proposal with their measured effect on every verified row (part 1 already has the
  table): longdyad 34.03, frontload 43.98, roller-flank 46.82, parallelogram 50.14, k-rim 71.68.
- seed-offset-3r-slides stays **gamed**: 3 passive translations breaks the user's hard rule. That is not a threshold
  choice.
